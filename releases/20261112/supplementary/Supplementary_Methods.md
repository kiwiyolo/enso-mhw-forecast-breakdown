# Scope of the supplementary analyses

These analyses address three questions: whether MHW forecast performance differs from historical expectations; how tropical and remote atmospheric forecasts differ; and how regional SST associations enter the diagnostic allocation of the regional map difference. Figures 1–4 and 16–17 assess MHW intensity, occurrence and sampling coverage; Figs. 5–11 examine tropical and remote atmospheric forecasts; Figs. 12–15 evaluate regional SST associations, their temporal transfer, a single-model process comparison and the signed map accounting. Supplementary Table 1 identifies the data and sampling support of each analysis.

# Events, repeated samples and uncertainty

The earlier strong events are 1997–1998 and 2015–2016; the focal event is 2023–2024. The nine-event amplitude regression additionally uses 1991–1992, 1994–1995, 2002–2003, 2004–2005, 2006–2007, 2009–2010 and 2018–2019. Month, lead, model and spatial-grid replication do not create additional independent ENSO events. In Fig. 2, the same 2,000 resampled calendar-year blocks are used jointly for both phases, yielding percentile intervals for correlations and their slope contrast. Prediction intervals in Fig. 1 describe a new event under the linear regression assumptions. Lead and historical-reference ranges elsewhere are descriptive ranges, not confidence intervals. No new multiple-comparison significance claims are made.

# AUC and conditional intensity errors

Global AUC in main Fig. 1a,b remains the original monthly spatial discrimination metric. The spatial sensitivity analyses in Supplementary Figs. 1c and 16–17 pool raw cell-month samples within an 11 × 11 array of 1° ocean grid cells (centre ±5 grid centres), restricted to the same major ocean basin and 60°S–60°N. Longitude wraps periodically. The primary window is April of the event year through September of the next year (18 months). Forecast records from the original July–June window are reused without modification; additional months use the same archived reader. Neighbour cells must have complete paired records for all events and leads. For each event and lead, weighted ROC AUC ranks forecast SST minus its local calendar-month Q90 against observed SST above Q90, with cosine-latitude sample weights and half credit for ties. A pool with only one observed class is undefined. Canonical-minus-target differences use equal weights for the two historical events and nine leads; only centres with all 27 finite AUCs enter the map. No interpolation or smoothing of the computed AUC is applied. Overlapping neighbourhoods reduce effective spatial independence despite the unchanged 1° output grid. Figure 17 separately checks 12 versus 18 months and single-cell versus 11° support. Forecast/observed intensity is max(SST − Q90,0); squared errors are pooled on observed-MHW samples only, using the same neighbourhood and time support. Roots follow MSE aggregation, including equal-event canonical averaging and region averaging. Regional neighbourhood metrics cannot be summed to recover the original global AUC gap. No claim of increased independent sample size is made.

# Forecast extraction shared with the main SST figures

Figures 1–4 and 15–17 retain the archived SST extraction used in main Figs. 1 and 4. That extraction averages stored initialization coordinates in some selected forecast files. A file-level audit has identified cases where filename-based and exact initialization-coordinate selections differ, including GFDL files for September and December 2023 with conflicting source-coordinate attribution. Consequently these supplementary SST panels describe the retained analysis, not an independently initialization-controlled validation. Exact-coordinate extraction changes the regression-based global deficit in the separately available sensitivity analysis; it is not silently substituted here. The magnitude of the SST-derived skill differences is therefore conditional on this extraction rule.

# Exact-coordinate DJF circulation comparisons

Figures 6–11 use DJF targets and exact available future leads 1–8 for COLA–RSMAS–CCSM4 (members 1–10), NASA–GEOS-S2S (1–4) and GFDL–SPEAR (1–15). Equal weights are applied to members within a model, then to model fields. All required fields must be finite. NCEP–CFSv2 is not in this fixed source set because its 1997–1998 Z200 product is unavailable there; NASA limits the common exact lead range to 1–8. Each calendar-month climatology spans 1992–2020, excluding February 2018 uniformly after the corresponding NASA field failed the availability check, leaving 29 December, 29 January and 28 February samples. No target-event DJF month is removed. The spatial correlation centers each field before cosine-latitude-weighted covariance; RMSE retains amplitude and sign-independent error magnitude. Pooled tropical–remote associations include between-event differences; their within-event counterparts cannot be substituted for them.

# Region definitions

For main Figs. 3–4 and Supplementary Figs. 7 and 12–15, NOAA WOA major-basin identity is intersected with latitude bands: TP/TA, 23.5°S–23.5°N; NP/NA, 23.5°N–60°N; SP/SA, 60°S–23.5°S. IO spans the Indian Ocean from 60°S to its northern boundary. Grid-centre membership assigns −23.5° to the tropical band and +23.5° to the northern band, with no overlap. NP, SP, TA, NA, SA and IO are the six atmospheric recipient ocean masks. Global circulation comparisons additionally include land. The spatial sensitivity analyses in Supplementary Figs. 1c and 16–17 use the retained ±20° split; they are shown explicitly as a different support, not silently relabelled as the process regions.

# Historical SST association and reconstruction

Figures 12–13 use regional ERSST SST and ORAS5 mixed-layer temperature, surface heating and horizontal advection. The fitting record is January 1985–December 2020 (432 months). ENSO is a separate group; IOD, PDO, NAO and SAM form the other-mode group. Local variables are previous-month SST and mixed-layer temperature, contemporaneous net surface heating divided by mixed-layer heat capacity, and horizontal advection. Full-sample month effects and linear trend are removed for the in-sample variance allocation. Group Shapley values average marginal explained variance over group orders; nested Owen values split the local parent while preserving group membership. The unresolved fraction is 1 − full-model R². All shares sum to one. For forward reconstruction, the seasonal cycle, trend, predictor scaling and regression are trained only before each test block. Test-time contemporaneous observed predictors make this diagnostic reconstruction rather than an actionable forecast.

# CFSv2-only process check

Figure 14 uses a separately retrieved CFSv2 process archive, four members (00, 06, 12 and 18), DJF and leads 1, 3 and 6 months. Verification uses ERA5 upper-level circulation and ORAS5 net ocean surface heat flux. The circulation quantity is the departure from contemporaneous zonal mean, not the calendar-month anomaly used in main Fig. 3. Net surface flux is positive downward into the ocean. Squared errors are averaged across the three months and cosine-latitude-weighted common ocean cells before taking the root. Each RMSE is divided by the mean of the two earlier-event RMSEs at the same region and lead. The regional masks have been recomputed at ±23.5°. CFSv2 is also one component of the main SST ensemble; this is therefore a separately processed single-model consistency check, not a model-independent validation of the mechanism.

# Exact relationship between Supplementary Fig. 15 and main Fig. 4

Let d(g) = 0.7050185255 − AUC(g), where AUC(g) is the nine-lead mean of the local 18-month, 11°-neighbourhood AUC, and let a(g) be cosine-latitude area weights normalized over all evaluable map cells. For region b, L(b) = sum a(g) max[d(g),0] and H(b) = sum a(g) max[−d(g),0]. The common denominator is N = sum_b [L(b) − H(b)] = 0.1065143704. The positive and negative percentages are 100 L(b)/N and −100 H(b)/N. They sum to +120.790563% and −20.790563%, respectively, and hence 100% net. Historical SST-association weights w(b,k) sum to one within each region; component amounts are sum_b L(b)w(b,k) and −sum_b H(b)w(b,k). Local subcomponents replace, rather than duplicate, their parent allocation. The global reference is not an estimate of each grid cell's expected AUC. The map aggregate differs from main Fig. 1's globally pooled monthly spatial-AUC deficit (0.1090497372); these are distinct estimands, with different ranking samples and aggregation order. Exact closure verifies accounting, not causal attribution. Source tables provide regional and component crosswalks to main Fig. 4.
