# Figure captions and statistical documentation

## Figure 1 | Strong El Niño did not deliver the historically expected MHW forecast skill

**a**, Event-mean global MHW AUC against peak Niño3.4 anomaly. The historical regression excludes 2023–2024; shading denotes its 95% prediction interval. Event error bars are 95% circular three-month block-bootstrap intervals for twelve-month event means, not regression prediction intervals. Open grey points show other earlier El Niño events, filled dark-grey points the canonical strong El Niño events (1997–1998 and 2015–2016), and the vermilion star 2023–2024. The historical expectation is 0.705 and the observed AUC 0.596, a difference of −0.109. These comparisons remain documented here; the expected-value point, expected/observed value labels, difference annotation and comparison line are omitted from the graphic. The observed-event star and uncertainty bar remain. **b**, Target-aligned monthly global AUC and the aligned Niño3.4 anomaly strip (−2.5 to +2.5 °C); shading identifies the three strong-event periods. All ten event estimates and their uncertainty intervals are retained. This presentation-only revision preserves the archived historical benchmark; it does not recalculate its robustness.

## Figure 2 | Tropical atmospheric forecast correlations decline more than SST correlations

The colourbar label **Forecast − observed (% of observed anomaly magnitude)** denotes `100 × (forecast anomaly − observed anomaly) / area-weighted spatial RMS of the observed anomaly`. Observed anomaly magnitude is the area-weighted root-mean-square anomaly over the analysis domain. Positive values indicate overestimation, and negative values indicate underestimation of the observed anomaly. Only the displayed wording has changed; the normalization, sign convention and values are unchanged.

In Figure 3, **200-hPa geopotential height anomaly (m)** refers to height departures from the retained seasonal climatology. **200-hPa geopotential height / Forecast − observed (m)** denotes the forecast-minus-observed height difference: positive values indicate that the forecast pressure surface lies higher than observed, and negative values indicate that it lies lower. The labels spell out Z200; data and colour scales are unchanged.

**a**, Tropical Pacific forecast errors in SST, zonal wind stress and convection, for the earlier strong El Niño events (1997–1998 and 2015–2016, equally weighted) and 2023–2024. Convection is represented by precipitation. Signed errors are normalized by the observed anomaly field's area-weighted root-mean-square amplitude. Stippling indicates agreement in error sign among at least 75% of at least three available model-resolved products; wind stress does not meet the model-count criterion. **b**, Area-weighted, centred forecast–observation spatial correlations at the nine archived lead labels. Dark lines and grey shading indicate the historical mean and range; vermilion indicates 2023–2024. **c**, Relative correlation decline, `100 × (rH − r23) / rH`, where each event's correlation is first averaged across the nine leads and the two historical events are equally weighted to obtain `rH`. Open points and whiskers show the two single-historical-event reference results and their range, not confidence intervals. The dashed line denotes SST decline. All 81 correlations, three bars and six reference-specific values are retained. Cross-variable comparisons retain the archived variable-specific windows and model availability and are descriptive, not an additive decomposition of MHW skill loss.

## Figure 3 | Tropical response errors accompany remote-circulation forecast differences

**a**, DJF Z200 anomalies for the observed canonical composite (1997–1998 and 2015–2016, equally weighted), observed 2023–2024, and the forecast-minus-observed anomaly in 2023–2024. The first two maps share ±160 m; the third uses ±100 m. Arrows show Z200-derived geostrophic wind anomalies or errors (10 m s⁻¹ reference); equatorial arrows are omitted. Error-map contours show canonical observed anomalies at 40 m intervals, with negative contours dashed. Dashed ocean-region boundaries retain ±23.5° tropical limits; dotted lines mark 45°N/50°S. The Robinson projection matches Fig.4a; region boundaries are not changed by this projection update. **b**, Forecast–observation spatial correlations for tropical convection (precipitation proxy; horizontal axis) and remote Z200 (vertical axis). Colours distinguish three events and shapes six recipient regions. All 144 samples, including negative Z200 correlations, are retained, clustered within only three events. Lower tropical convection correlations tend to accompany lower remote Z200 correlations; the pooled descriptive association is 0.62. The dashed fit and whole-event resampling envelope do not establish causal propagation or precise inferential coverage. **c**, Cosine-latitude-weighted, centred spatial correlations of DJF mean Z200 anomalies over 60°S–60°N and all longitudes, including land and ocean. Each forecast composite is compared with three observed references. “Other El Niño” equally combines 1994–1995, 2002–2003, 2004–2005, 2006–2007, 2009–2010 and 2018–2019. Canonical combines 1997–1998 and 2015–2016. All six descriptive correlations use 43,560 native 1° nodes; no confidence intervals or significance tests are introduced. Bars start at zero; blank space above 1 accommodates the internal legend.

For **Fig.3b**, the horizontal axis is tropical convection correlation (`r`), and the vertical axis is Z200 forecast correlation (`r`); both are forecast–observation spatial correlations, and the former uses precipitation as the convection proxy. Both axis titles are shown. DJF denotes December–February; the dotted 45°N/50°S lines, canonical 40 m contours and geostrophic anomaly/error arrows remain as described above.

## Methods/SI wording retained with the figures

“Canonical strong El Nino” in Figs.1–2 denotes the same two historical events, 1997–1998 and 2015–2016. Fig.2 shows their equal-weight average and their two-event range; its definition has not changed with the label.

The following Fig.3 descriptions are retained in the caption instead of the graphic: maps show December–February (DJF); dotted lines mark 45°N and 50°S; contours show observed canonical Z200 anomalies at 40 m intervals; arrows show geostrophic wind anomalies in the observed maps and geostrophic wind errors in the forecast-error map; panel c covers 60°S–60°N and all longitudes. Axis ticks, all data points, arrows, contours and reference lines remain on the graphic.

Panel b now displays the archived `Rain_r` and `Z200_r` columns directly, after checking they equal one minus their former error coordinates. The same transformation is applied to the existing fitted line and resampling envelope: `x_new = 1 − x_old`, `fit_new = 1 − fit_old`, `lower_new = 1 − upper_old`, and `upper_new = 1 − lower_old`. This reverses both error axes into correlation axes, preserving the pooled association and all samples. The fitted resampling envelope extends slightly above r = 1 because it is an unconstrained regression envelope; no individual correlation exceeds 1, and no new inference is introduced.

Convection in Figs.2–3 refers to the precipitation-based diagnostic, not directly measured convective heating or divergent circulation. The displayed label is simplified; the underlying variable is unchanged. Forecast errors and spatial correlations retain their source definitions and windows. The Fig.2 historical-relative decline is a ratio of aggregated correlations, not a relative RMSE or a mean of lead-specific ratios. Its whiskers quantify reference-event sensitivity. Fig.3b's repeated leads/regions must be treated as clustered within events. Fig.3c correlates event- and season-composited anomaly fields, rather than averaging regional correlations; “Other El Niño” is not climatology. Full calculation contracts and original caveats remain in `provenance/`.

No new hypothesis tests, P values, multiple-testing adjustments, independent-event claims, significance markers or uncertainty intervals were added in this cosmetic revision. Fig.3c contains deterministic composite statistics and has no error bars to infer. Source-matched intervals in Figs.1 and2 and the event-resampling envelope in Fig.3b are preserved exactly.
